Easy Minestrone Soup

Watch on YouTube
Time ~55 min
Serves 4–6
Source Steve Cusato

Ingredients

Soup base

  • 1–2 carrots, diced
  • 2 celery stalks, diced
  • 1 onion, diced
  • 5–6 garlic cloves, minced
  • 5–6 kale leaves, washed and sliced
  • 1 can (28 oz / 800 g) whole tomatoes, pureed or crushed
  • 1 cup (240 ml) cannellini beans
  • ¼ small cabbage, roughly chopped
  • 1 quart (1 L) brodo (broth) or stock
  • 1 Parmigiano rind
  • 1 bay leaf
  • 3 thyme sprigs
  • 1–2 tbsp chili flakes
  • 2–3 tbsp olive oil
  • Salt and black pepper, to taste

Optional

  • 1 cup (150 g) pancetta, diced (optional)

Pasta

  • ⅔ cup (about 90 g) ditalini (~¼ cup per person, depending on portions)

To serve

  • Grated Parmesan
  • Good-quality finishing olive oil
  • Chopped fresh parsley

Method

1
Dice all vegetables and pancetta into roughly the same size.
2
In a large pot over medium heat, add the pancetta with a little olive oil and the chili flakes (if using). Render out the fat and brown the meat. Once browned, add the diced vegetables and season lightly with salt to draw out moisture. Sweat — not colour — until soft and translucent.
3
Add the brodo or stock, crushed tomatoes, herbs, bay leaf, kale, beans and the Parmigiano rind. Stir to combine. Bring to a boil, then reduce to a gentle simmer. Cook until the vegetables are tender but still have a little bite — about 40 minutes. Taste and adjust salt as needed.
4
Cook the pasta separately: if serving the whole pot at once, boil the ditalini in salted water and add to individual bowls. If eating in portions, ladle the amount you'll serve into a smaller pot, thin with a splash of water if needed, then cook the pasta directly in that portion — the soup will thicken as the pasta absorbs liquid. Cook until the pasta is al dente.
5
Serve in bowls, topped with grated Parmesan, a drizzle of finishing olive oil and chopped parsley.

Notes

  • This is a flexible soup — use whatever vegetables you have on hand. The core is the soffritto (carrot, celery, onion, garlic) and the stock; everything else adapts.
  • A serving is approximately 2–2½ cups of soup.
  • Cook the pasta in a separate portion of soup (not the whole pot) if you plan to store leftovers. Pasta cooked directly in the full pot will turn mushy on reheating.
  • If the soup tastes flat, salt is usually the fix — add a little at a time until the flavours brighten.
  • The Parmigiano rind adds a lot of depth; don't skip it if you have one.

A classic Italian peasant soup built on the idea of using whatever's available. Steve Cusato keeps it simple and adaptable — the point is to load up on vegetables, let the Parmigiano rind do its quiet work, and not overthink it. The pancetta is optional but adds a smoky backbone; without it, the soup is fully vegetarian. The trick with the pasta is practical: cook it only in the portion you're eating now, so the rest keeps well in the fridge without turning to mush.
