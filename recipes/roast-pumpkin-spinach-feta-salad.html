Roast Pumpkin, Spinach and Feta Salad

Watch on YouTube
Time 35 min
Serves 4–5
Source Nagi Maehashi

Ingredients

Pumpkin

  • 600 g (1.2 lb) pumpkin (after peeling), cut into 3 cm (1¼") cubes
  • 1½ tbsp olive oil
  • Salt and pepper

Dressing

  • 50 ml (2½ tbsp) extra virgin olive oil
  • 2 tbsp balsamic vinegar
  • 1 tbsp honey
  • Salt and pepper

Salad

  • 35 g (¼ cup) pine nuts
  • 150 g (5 oz) baby spinach leaves (4 handfuls)
  • 60 g (2 oz) feta, crumbled (or more!!)

Method

1
Preheat oven to 220°C (430°F) standard or 200°C (390°F) fan/convection.
2
Toss pumpkin with olive oil, salt and pepper. Spread on baking tray, bake for 20 minutes. Remove from oven, flip, then bake for a further 7–10 minutes until golden but not mushy. Loosen pumpkin with an egg flip (it can adhere as it cools), then if serving the salad at room temperature, leave to cool.
3
Shake Dressing in a jar. Taste and adjust. I like it a bit sharp because it balances out the flavours in this salad.
4
Toast pine nuts in a dry skillet over medium heat until light golden and it smells nutty. Remove pine nuts from skillet as soon as they're ready.
5
Place spinach in a bowl. Drizzle with a bit of dressing then toss.
6
Add pumpkin, just a bit of feta and pine nuts, then GENTLY, BRIEFLY toss just to disperse the feta. (If you toss vigorously, the feta can make it look messy.)
7
Transfer to serving plate. Sprinkle over remaining feta and pine nuts. Just before serving, drizzle with remaining dressing (dressing doesn't stay on baby spinach well, so it's worth drizzling at the end). Serve!

Notes

  • You can use any pumpkin or squash for this recipe.
  • Pine nuts are expensive but worth using here – they're the perfect finishing touch. You can sub with any other nuts of choice; chopped almonds and macadamias are great.
  • Pumpkin turns to mush if baked for too long. It should be tender with a nice golden surface on at least 2 sides (the sides in contact with the tray). Loosen it from the tray with an egg flip / spatula as soon as it comes out of the oven – if you don't do this while hot, it might stick.

Nagi pitches this as a make-ahead weekend lunch that works both as a side and as a meal on its own. The honey balsamic dressing is deliberately kept a little sharp to balance the sweetness of the roast pumpkin, and because it slides off baby spinach, most of it goes on right at the end rather than in the toss.
