Chicken Tikka Masala

Watch on YouTube
Time ~1 hr
Serves 4–5
Source Nagi Maehashi

Ingredients

Chicken tikka marinade

  • 600 g (1.2 lb) boneless, skinless chicken thigh, cut into 3 cm (2.2") cubes
  • ½ cup full-fat plain yoghurt (Greek is fine)
  • 6 cloves garlic, minced (~1½ tbsp)
  • 1 tbsp fresh ginger, grated
  • 1 tsp garam masala
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 1 tsp cumin
  • 1 tsp ground coriander
  • 1 tsp paprika (sweet/smoked)
  • 1 tbsp vegetable oil
  • ⅛ tsp cayenne pepper (optional)
  • Pinch black pepper
  • 2 tsp lemon juice

Cooking the chicken

  • 1–2 tbsp oil, high smoke point

Curry sauce spices

  • 2 tsp turmeric
  • 1 tbsp garam masala
  • 2 tsp ground coriander
  • 1 tsp cumin
  • ⅛ tsp cardamom powder
  • ⅛ tsp cayenne pepper

Curry sauce

  • 3 tbsp (65 ml) vegetable oil
  • 30 g (2 tbsp) unsalted butter or ghee
  • 1 onion, finely chopped
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 2 tbsp fresh ginger, grated
  • 6 cloves garlic, crushed or grated
  • 1 tbsp paprika (not smoked)
  • 1⅔ cups (400 ml) tomato passata
  • 1⅔ cups (400 ml) water
  • 100 ml (⅓ cup + 1 tbsp) cream
  • 1 tsp sugar
  • 50 g (3 tbsp) unsalted butter or ghee

Serving

  • Basmati rice

Method

1
Combine marinade ingredients, excluding chicken, and mix well. Add chicken and coat evenly.
2
Cover and marinate in the fridge for at least 3 hours, preferably overnight.
3
Heat 1 tbsp oil in a non-stick pan over high heat until smoking. Add half the chicken in a single layer; leave 2 minutes until charred. Turn and char the second side (the interior will finish cooking later). Transfer to a bowl.
4
Discard any burnt residue in the pan, add more oil if needed, and cook the remaining chicken the same way.
5
Wipe out the skillet with paper towel. Reduce heat to medium-high, add the oil and butter for the sauce. Once the butter melts, add onion, ginger, and salt.
6
Stir constantly until the ginger turns golden and the onion smells sweet, about 5–7 minutes.
7
Reduce heat to medium. Add garlic and paprika; cook 2 minutes.
8
Add the curry sauce spices and cook a further 2 minutes, stirring.
9
Add the tomato passata and water; stir to combine. Bring to a simmer, cover, and reduce heat to low. Simmer 15 minutes, stirring occasionally.
10
Pour the curry into a bowl and blend with a stick blender until completely smooth.
11
Return the sauce to the skillet. Add cream, sugar, and butter; stir until the butter melts.
12
Add the chicken and stir. Simmer a few minutes until the chicken is fully cooked through.
13
Optional: sprinkle with extra garam masala. Serve over basmati rice.

Notes

  • Use chicken thigh, not breast — the high heat needed for charring will overcook and dry out breast meat.
  • Use a neutral, high smoke point oil (canola, coconut, grapeseed) — not olive oil.
  • Try to keep the charred coating on the chicken, but don't worry if some sticks to the pan — you still get the flavour. A well-seasoned cast iron pan chars even better than non-stick.
  • Don't skip blending the sauce — puréeing the caramelised onion, garlic and ginger with everything else is what gives it that silky, restaurant-style texture. Never blend it piping hot in a sealed blender.
  • Don't skimp on the oil and butter in the sauce — it's what gives the dish its richness. Even at full quantity it's only about 1.7 tbsp per serving.
  • Chicken can marinate up to 48 hours, or be frozen in the marinade and thawed overnight. Cooked curry keeps 3 days in the fridge or 3 months frozen — add a pinch of garam masala when reheating to freshen the aroma.
  • Kenji López-Alt's version (Serious Eats) makes a point of deliberately undercooking the chicken when charring it — pulling it off the heat while the inside is still essentially raw, then finishing it in the simmering sauce. The logic carries over here too: the char step above is about building colour and smoke flavour, not cooking the chicken through — that happens in step 12. Don't worry if the chicken still looks underdone after charring.
  • Also from Kenji: letting the onion, garlic and ginger catch a little colour or char in spots (rather than just going golden) adds a smokier, sweeter depth to the sauce that pairs well with the charred chicken. Worth trying as a variation on step 6.
  • Kenji splits the fresh cilantro in two: half goes in with the tomatoes and gets blended into the sauce (adds an earthy background note), the other half goes in fresh at the end with a squeeze of lemon (keeps the bright, citrusy top note that cooking destroys). Could add ½ cup chopped cilantro leaves and tender stems this way — half in step 9, half stirred in at step 13.

This recipe is the result of a mountain of reading, researching, tasting and cooking in pursuit of the ultimate Tikka Masala for the home cook — visiting Sydney's Indian restaurants just to taste theirs, comparing notes against Madhur Jaffrey and Heston Blumenthal, Hindi-language YouTube videos, and faded old cookbooks dug up from the library, then pulling the best bits from each source.
