Asparagus, Ricotta and Mint Tart

Time ~45 min
Serves 4–6
Source Julius Roberts

Ingredients

  • 250 g ricotta
  • 50 g crumbly goat’s cheese (or parmesan if you prefer)
  • 1 unwaxed lemon
  • a bunch of fresh mint (about 20 g), leaves picked and finely chopped
  • a bunch of fresh chives (about 20 g), finely chopped
  • 1 sheet of puff pastry
  • 15–20 asparagus spears
  • olive oil
  • 1 egg

Method

1
Preheat your oven to 200°C fan (220°C conventional).
2
Place the ricotta in a bowl with the goat’s cheese and season well with salt and pepper. Grate in the zest of the whole lemon and add the juice of half. Whisk together until smooth and creamy, then add the herbs, holding back about a third for serving. Mix again, then taste and adjust the seasoning as necessary.
3
Roll out the puff pastry on a baking tray lined with baking parchment. With a knife, score the pastry 3–4 cm from the edge all the way round — this allows the edges to rise and puff up. Be careful not to cut all the way through.
4
Snap the asparagus at the bottom to remove the woody ends.
5
Spread the ricotta over the pastry, taking care to stay within the scored border. Top with the spears of asparagus, drizzle with olive oil and season well.
6
Whisk the egg and brush the edges of the pastry so they go golden in the oven.
7
Bake for 20–30 minutes, until crisp and puffed up.
8
Out of the oven, sprinkle with the last of the herbs, squeeze over some lemon juice and finish with a drizzle of olive oil.

Notes

  • The herbs are interchangeable — wild garlic is fantastic, as are basil or dill. Mint and chives are the first hardy herbs up in the garden in early spring, which is why they are used so abundantly here.
  • Save the woody asparagus ends over the course of the season and use them to make soup.
  • The book holds back only a small handful of herbs for serving — hold back about a third of the mint instead. Folded into the ricotta it is shielded from the heat, but it still flattens out; the mint added at the end is the one you actually taste.
  • The final drizzle of olive oil is not in the book — it comes from J. Kenji López-Alt’s asparagus tartine (Serious Eats), where the oil goes on both before and after. It lifts the whole thing.
  • The book says to serve it immediately while piping hot. It is just as good warm or at room temperature. If it has to wait — a buffet, a picnic — whisk 1 egg into the ricotta mixture, as The Baking Explorer does, and brush the edges with milk instead: the filling sets and the tart slices cleanly once cool.

Julius Roberts calls this more than the sum of its parts, and the reason is structural: there is no blind baking and no pastry faff. The scored border does all the work — it rises into a rim while the weight of the ricotta holds the centre flat, so a single sheet of ready-made puff becomes a tart in one bake. Everything else is early-spring produce handled as little as possible.
