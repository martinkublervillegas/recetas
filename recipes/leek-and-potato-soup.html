Leek and Potato Soup

Time ~40 min
Serves 6
Source Heston Blumenthal

Ingredients

For the soup

  • 180 g peeled and very finely sliced waxy potatoes (approx. 2 medium potatoes)
  • 120 g unsalted butter
  • 200 g peeled and very finely sliced onion (approx. 3 medium onions)
  • 750 g cleaned and very finely sliced leek (approx. 15 leeks)
  • 1 bouquet garni (thyme, celery leaves and parsley, tied together with a strip of leek or a piece of string)
  • 1 kg vegetable stock, or white chicken stock, warmed
  • 100 g double cream
  • 50 g semi-skimmed milk
  • Salt

To serve

  • Chives

Method

1
Rinse the sliced potatoes under cold running water for a couple of minutes to remove the starch which might otherwise make the soup thick and gluey.
2
Melt the butter in a large saucepan and sweat the onion and potato over a medium-high heat for 10 minutes. Add the finely sliced leek and cook for a further 5 minutes, or just until the raw leek smell has disappeared.
3
Add the bouquet garni and the warmed stock to the pan, bring to the boil and reduce to a simmer. Add the cream and milk, and simmer for 10 minutes or until the potatoes are tender.
4
Remove the bouquet garni and liquidize the soup, then pass through a fine sieve into a clean pan. If necessary use a little more stock to thin out the soup.
5
Season with salt and warm through. Aerate the soup with a hand blender to make it frothy and light, then ladle into warm bowls. Chop the chives and garnish each bowl.

Notes

  • Leeks lose a lot of their colour and flavour if cooked for longer than about 20 minutes, so slice them finely to cook them as fast as possible. The onions and potatoes need longer to soften, which is why they get a head start in the pan.
  • Include some of the green part of the leek — it gives a better colour.
  • Add the cream 5–10 minutes before the end, so it has time to cook and lose its raw lactic note.
  • Vegetable stock keeps the soup vegetarian; white chicken stock makes it better if you aren't serving vegetarians.

Heston Blumenthal at Home was the book where he tried to move the Fat Duck's method — not its theatre — into a domestic kitchen, and a soup this humble is a good place to see what that means. Nothing here is exotic; what is unusual is how much of the recipe is about sequence and temperature rather than ingredients. The potatoes are rinsed before they ever meet heat, the leek goes in last and briefly, the cream cooks rather than merely warms, and the last thing that happens is not a garnish but a hand blender run through the pot for texture.
