Puy Lentils with Spinach & Pancetta

Time ~1 h
Serves 6
Source Julius Roberts

Ingredients

  • 1 large brown onion
  • 1 leek
  • 2 carrots
  • 3 celery sticks
  • 4 tbsp olive oil
  • 150 g diced pancetta (optional)
  • A generous glass of white wine
  • 500 g Puy lentils
  • 5 bay leaves
  • A few sprigs of fresh thyme
  • 1 litre chicken stock (see page 306), veg stock or water
  • 500 g spinach
  • 1 heaped tbsp Dijon mustard, plus more to taste
  • A big handful of fresh parsley
  • Salt and pepper

Method

1
Finely dice the onion, leek, carrots and celery. Heat the olive oil in a large pan, then add the veg and the pancetta, if using. Season well and cook gently for 15–20 minutes, until soft and sweet.
2
Add the wine and cook off the alcohol, then rinse the lentils in a sieve and add to the pan, giving them a good stir.
3
Follow with the bay leaves, thyme and stock, adding just enough to barely cover the lentils. Bring to a gentle simmer and cook for around 30 minutes, until the lentils are tender and beginning to open but still have a nice bit of bite to them.
4
When ready, add the spinach and cook until barely wilted, then take off the heat, add the mustard and the finely chopped parsley.
5
Give it a good stir and taste to check your seasoning — it'll need a hefty pinch of salt and pepper, and often some more mustard, so adjust as necessary.

Notes

  • Pictured with pumpkin roasted in a hot oven until sweet and charred, and finished with goat's cheese.
  • Particularly good with quality sausages and a mustardy aïoli, but equally so with pan-fried fish and salsa verde, or slow-roast lamb and anchovy.
  • Leftovers make a great pasta sauce finished with a drizzle of chilli oil and parmesan — or water them down a bit, add some curry powder and turn them into curried lentil soup.
  • Keeps in the fridge for a good few days — just make sure to reheat it properly.
  • Puy lentils can be swapped for black (beluga) lentils or any other lentil that holds its shape when cooked. Avoid red or split lentils, which break down into a purée.
