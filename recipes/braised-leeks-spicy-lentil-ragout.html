Braised Leeks with Spicy Lentil Ragout

Time ~50 min
Serves 4
Source Jesse Jenkins

Ingredients

  • 3–4 leeks
  • 1–2 carrots, peeled and diced
  • 1 onion, diced
  • 2 stalks celery, diced
  • 200 g black lentils
  • 1 tbsp tomato paste
  • 1 tbsp gochujang
  • 900 ml stock — 700 ml for the lentils, 200 ml for the leeks (I used beef stock)
  • A splash of white wine for the leeks, or use the stock (optional)
  • 1 tbsp sherry vinegar
  • 2 tbsp unsalted butter
  • 1 handful chives or parsley, or both
  • Crème fraîche, to taste
  • Salt and pepper, to taste
  • Lemon juice, for the leeks

Method

1
Clean the leeks. Trim at least two or three of the outer layers — they almost always have grit more than halfway down — then trim the tops and leave them in a bowl under running water.
2
Cut the leeks into 1 to 2 inch (2.5–5 cm) rounds and sear them in a pan over medium heat with enough oil to lightly coat the bottom. Sear for about five minutes until you can see the colour creeping around the edges, then season with salt and add the white wine or stock. Cover with a lid, turn down the heat and cook for about 10 minutes, keeping an eye on the pan so the liquid doesn't completely reduce and they begin to burn.
3
If you don't see any steam sputtering out from the sides of the lid, you can assume they're about to start burning very quickly. Add a little more liquid if they're not soft enough. When they're done, turn off the heat and squeeze fresh lemon all over.
4
In a large saucepan or casserole add a few splashes of oil along with the carrots, onion and celery and a big pinch of salt. Sauté until just softened, about 10 minutes, making sure not to get any colour. Add a tablespoon each of tomato paste and gochujang and cook for 2 to 3 minutes.
5
Add the rinsed lentils and the stock. Bring to a simmer and leave to gently bubble away for 20 to 30 minutes, checking around the 20 minute mark to see how tender they are. Cook them uncovered; you usually don't need any extra liquid, but if they start to dry out before they're ready just add a few splashes at a time.
6
Finish the lentils with a glug or two of good sherry vinegar, a couple knobs of butter and a big handful of chives or parsley, or both.
7
Gently warm the leeks before topping the lentils with them. Finish with a generous scoop of crème fraîche.

Notes

  • If you're not keeping the leeks whole, the easiest way to clean them is to cut them in half, slice them, then rinse and strain a couple of times. Trimming the outer layers is what it takes when you want to keep the rounds intact.
  • Losing a layer or two feels like a lot of waste, but it's just the nature of this vegetable. The trim can go into stocks and soups — clean it off first.

Jenkins cooks vegetables the way he learned to in professional kitchens: two or three stages, layering flavour and texture, then something at the end that pulls it together. This plate is that method in miniature — the leeks and the ragout are built separately, in their own pans, and only meet once each is finished, with vinegar, butter and crème fraîche doing the joining.
